    How to use this app

    Six steps, from a supplier price list to a selling price.

    1 Put your raw materials in the price list

    Open the Ingredients tab. Every material you buy goes in here once.

    1. The name you know the material by.
    2. The price you pay your supplier.
    3. What that price buys — per kg, L, pcs and so on. Enter the price exactly as it is quoted; the app works out the rest.
    4. Density, only if you buy a material by litre but use it by weight (or the other way round). Leave it empty otherwise.
    5. Adds an empty row. Everything saves as you type.

    2 Create the product

    Go to Formulations and press + New, then describe the batch you make.

    1. The product name, as you want it on the cost sheet.
    2. How much you make in one batch — for example 100 kg.
    3. How much goes in one jar or bottle. 100 kg ÷ 50 g = 2,000 units.
    4. How much material is lost in the vessel and the filling line. 2 % means you buy 2 % more than the recipe says.

    3 Write the recipe

    Add one row per ingredient. Costs appear while you type.

    1. An optional phase label (A, B, C…) — it only groups the cost sheet.
    2. Pick the material from your price list.
    3. How much of it: a percentage of the batch, or a real quantity if you switch the toggle above.
    4. What that comes to in kilos and in money, for this batch.
    5. The running total. It must reach 100 % — the badge turns amber until it does.

    4 Add the other costs and your profit

    1. The jar, cap, carton and label for one unit.
    2. What making one batch costs you in wages.
    3. Rent, electricity, QC — as a percentage of everything above.
    4. How much you add on top of cost. 120 % means you sell for 2.2 × what it cost you.
    5. VAT, added at the very end.

    5 Read your price

    1. What one batch costs you in total.
    2. What one jar costs you.
    3. What you sell it for, before and after VAT.
    4. The same figures written out, so you can see where the money goes.

    If anything is wrong — percentages that do not reach 100 %, a material with no price — a coloured note appears at the bottom of this panel and tells you what to fix.

    6 Keep a backup

    Your data lives in this browser only. It is not sent anywhere.

    1. The currency everything is shown in. Prices are never converted — type them all in the same currency.
    2. Saves everything to one file. Do this regularly.
    3. Loads a file you saved before, replacing what is here.
    4. Brings back the example products, if you want something to look at.

    To print or save a cost sheet as PDF, open a product and press Print cost sheet.
